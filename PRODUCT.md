# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

A mixed audience of employers, recruiters, potential clients, and the creative community. Each arrives with a different intent — hiring fit, project commission, or appreciation of craft — but all should leave with the same conviction: this is a developer who treats UI as an art form. The portfolio speaks first to people who recognize intentional design when they see it.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio for Muhammad Reza Ananta that communicates developer-with-design-taste positioning through a visual language inspired by Persona 5's bold, stylish, rebellious spirit — without veering into gaming-fan-site territory. Success means the visitor feels compelled to reach out, whether to hire, commission, or follow the work. The single strategic claim: style meets substance.

## Positioning

A developer who bridges code and visual craft, where every pixel on the page is as intentional as every line in the build. Not a standard developer portfolio (white bg, tidy cards, blue accent). Not a gaming shrine. Something that feels edited, confident, and unmistakably deliberate.

## Conversion & proof

- **Primary CTA**: View the work / projects. The portfolio pieces are the main event — each project is a self-contained argument for the creator's skill.
- **Secondary CTA**: Contact / hire. For visitors convinced by the work.
- **The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds**: "Style meets substance — this is what intentional design looks like."
- **Belief ladder**:
  1. This person has taste and technical skill.
  2. Their work is intentional and crafted, not templated.
  3. They can bring that same intentionality to your project.
  4. You should reach out and work with them.
- **Proof on hand**: Past projects and case studies, to be detailed in each portfolio entry.

## Brand Personality

Bold. Stylish. Rebellious. The personality draws from Persona 5's rebel-chic confidence — sharp contrast, theatrical framing, and a sense that the layout is making a statement — but translated into professional design language. The feeling should be "this is special" rather than "this is a game."

## Anti-references

- A standard developer portfolio (white background, tidy card grid, blue accent, resume-template energy).
- A gaming fan site (character art, game UI chrome, interface that sacrifices readability for theme).
- SaaS landing pages (hero metrics, gradient CTAs, cookie-cutter conversion layouts).

The goal is a third lane: a portfolio that feels editorial, authored, and confident, drawing energy from Persona 5's design philosophy — not its iconography.

## Design Principles

1. **Intentionality over ornament.** Every element earns its place. If it doesn't contribute to the argument or the experience, remove it.
2. **High contrast, high confidence.** Let the bold red-and-black palette carry the weight. Don't soften it with gradients, shadows, or decorative blurs.
3. **Theatrical but not literal.** Channel Persona 5's energy through layout rhythm, typographic weight shifts, and punctuated motion — not through game UI patterns or character motifs.
4. **Show, don't tell.** The work is the proof. Each project page is a self-contained experience that demonstrates craft.
5. **Accessibility is design.** WCAG AA contrast, reduced motion support, and color-blind safe palettes are not constraints — they sharpen the final result.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum (4.5:1 body contrast, keyboard navigable, screen reader compatible).
- Reduced motion support — all animations degrade gracefully to crossfade or instant.
- Color-blind safe palette — the red/black contrast is tested for protanopia and deuteranopia.
